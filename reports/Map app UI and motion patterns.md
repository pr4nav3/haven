# Subtract first, then animate with precision

The products people describe as "premium" in map and motion-heavy categories earn that reputation by removing things, not by adding motion: Uber's home screen is one question ("Where to?"), Watch Duty's first screen is a map and nothing else, Noonlight's is a single hold button, and Google Maps and Apple Maps have both converged on sheets that never fully cover the map. Their motion, where it is documented at all, is short (**under 300 ms** for feedback), ease-out, low-bounce, origin-anchored, interruptible, and reserved for moments that carry information (Emil Kowalski, Material 3 tokens, Apple's WWDC23 spring guidance, Vaul's iOS-matched `cubic-bezier(0.32, 0.72, 0, 1)` at 0.5 s). Their visual systems on dark maps share one structure: near-black neutral land in the `#0e0e0e`–`#1f1f1f` band, water only a few steps lighter and slightly blue, roads as light strokes separated by land-colored casings in roughly 10-unit lightness steps, labels never pure white, and a single blue route (`#56A8FB` over `#2F7AC6` casing in Mapbox Navigation) kept far from the red/amber alert hues. Nobody publishes measured camera durations, route-draw timings, or user-dot colors for a shipping app, so those values must be derived from platform defaults and animation tokens, which this report does explicitly. For Haven, a one-button "get me to the nearest shelter" web app for Wisconsin, the evidence points to a single screen with three states (idle, routing, guiding), one full-width near-white button that is the brightest object on screen, a bottom sheet that peeks at roughly 12% and never exceeds 92% height, a thin non-blocking severity banner at the top, a lifted-contrast fork of OpenFreeMap Dark, a `line-gradient` step route reveal in MapLibre, a canvas-image pulsing dot, and a fallback ladder (precise GPS, coarse ring, saved address, dropped pin, regional list) that never dead-ends. Every number in the closing spec is traced to a source or labelled as a derivation, and the main open risk, dark-map legibility in direct sunlight, is handled with lifted stroke contrast plus an explicit light-theme toggle rather than wished away.

## Every praised map app ships one labelled action over a bare map

Across ride-hail, transit and navigation, the home screen that reviewers praise is a map, a user dot, one labelled call-to-action near the thumb, and at most one or two floating round buttons. Uber's 2016 redesign replaced its map-first home with a single question, later admitted feature bloat, and its February 2023 refresh re-simplified around "Where to?" with saved destinations and personalised suggestions ([Mobile Marketing Magazine](https://mobilemarketingmagazine.com/uber-redesigns-app-focusing-on-a-single-question/); [RideGuru](https://ride.guru/content/newsroom/ubers-new-redesign-explained)). Lyft won a Core77 Interaction Award for cutting booking to **three taps**, keeping key interactions at the bottom for one-handed reach and pushing secondary information into an expanding panel ([Core77](https://designawards.core77.com/Interaction/87383/Rider-App-Redesign)); its 2023 map work then stripped "the distracting clutter of irrelevant stores, restaurants, and nearby destinations" from the basemap itself ([Lyft blog](https://www.lyft.com/blog/posts/lyfts-secret-plan-to-take-control-of-its-maps-and-its-future)). Citymapper's labelled "Get Me Somewhere" bar is credited with signalling exactly what the system affords, and its Home/Work/Places shortcuts are praised as recognition over recall; the same critique's one complaint is a small, low-contrast "Leave: Now" control floating over map imagery, which users miss ([IXD@Pratt, Feb 2026](https://ixd.prattsi.org/2026/02/design-critique-citymapper-ios-app/)). That pairing of praise and complaint is the clearest single lesson for Haven: labelled, high-contrast, bottom-anchored controls are loved; small floating controls over map tiles are not.

The two incumbents have converged on a specific rule about sheets. Google Maps' 2024–2025 directions redesign moved the transport-mode carousel to the bottom for reachability, shrank the results card so "a sliver of map" stays visible at the top, and keeps a strip of map behind the status bar even when a place sheet is fully expanded ([9to5Google, Feb 2024](https://9to5google.com/2024/02/07/google-maps-directions-search-redesign/); [9to5Google, Mar 2025](https://9to5google.com/2025/03/07/google-maps-iphone-sheet-redesign/)). Apple Maps in iOS 26 made its search bar and menus translucent so the map shows through, replaced the full-width turn banner with a smaller pop-up bar, and keeps a single floating Directions button as the one persistent control ([MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/guide/ios-26-maps)). The Apple Maps sheet, as reproduced in detail by Expo, uses **three detents at 0.1 / 0.5 / 1.0** of screen height, starts at the lowest, floats with a visible gap and full corner radius at low detents, docks at the top, shows a grabber, and disables drag-to-dismiss ([Expo blog](https://expo.dev/blog/how-to-create-apple-maps-style-liquid-glass-sheets)). The grabber itself is cited by multiple iOS developers as the model drag affordance ([Sarunw](https://sarunw.com/posts/bottom-sheet-in-ios-15-with-uisheetpresentationcontroller/)), and a draggable-sheet guide warns that non-modal sheets without a clear affordance cause users to stall ([vp0.com](https://vp0.com/blogs/draggable-bottom-sheet-map-modal)). The practical reading is that "the map is always partly visible" is now the expected norm, so a web sheet should cap its top detent below 100%.

The emergency-app evidence pushes the same way but harder. Watch Duty's design brief was to deliver rich, fast-changing fire information "without being overwhelming to people already under stress," and reviewers reward exactly that with phrases like "no clicking and looking around" and "clean and uncomplicated" ([Andrew Liebchen](https://linkedin.com/in/andrewliebchen); [JustUseApp](https://justuseapp.com/en/app/1574452924/watch-duty-wildfire/reviews)). Citizen is the cautionary case: its original first screen was "a dark map that sprouts red dots," which its own head of product conceded "can certainly create a lot of paranoia and anxiety," so the redesign replaced the feed with a summary of the user's immediate surroundings and a stated goal that users "check it and close it" ([AIGA Eye on Design](https://eyeondesign.aiga.org/do-crime-tracking-apps-have-a-design-problem/)). NERV's home shows only what is relevant to the user's current time and place ([NERV App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/nerv-disaster-prevention/id1472338480)); Noonlight's is one hold-to-cancel button ([WhistleOut](https://www.whistleout.com/CellPhones/Guides/noonlight-panic-button-for-solo-travel-safety)); Apple's Emergency SOS via satellite walks through a questionnaire answered "with simple taps" ([Apple support](https://support.apple.com/en-us/101573)). The hold-plus-countdown pattern that Apple, Life360 and Noonlight share exists because those actions contact other people; it is the wrong pattern for a routing tap, which is non-destructive and should fire on a single press with no delay. NN/g's analysis of the 2018 Hawaii false alert adds the corollary that any action that does contact others must be visually separated from the routing button so "a slip of the finger" cannot cross between them ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/error-prevention/)).

## Motion stays under 300 ms, eases out, and never bounces for status

The strongest convergence in the motion notes is a ceiling. Emil Kowalski, whose Vaul and Sonner components are the de facto web sheet and toast, states that UI animations "should also usually be shorter than 300ms," that easing is "the most important part of any animation," that the default should be ease-out, that ease-in is never appropriate for UI, and that CSS keyword curves "are usually not strong enough" beyond simple hovers ([emilkowal.ski, great animations](https://emilkowal.ski/ui/great-animations); [good vs great](https://emilkowal.ski/ui/good-vs-great-animations)). Material 3's token file encodes the same philosophy numerically: durations from **50 ms to 1000 ms** in fixed steps, a standard/emphasized curve of `cubic-bezier(0.2, 0, 0, 1)`, decelerate curves for entering and accelerate curves for exiting ([material-web tokens](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/material-components/material-web/main/tokens/versions/v0_192/_md-sys-motion.scss)). Apple's spring API defaults to **bounce 0**, which the WWDC23 session calls "a great general purpose spring"; about 0.15 is barely perceptible, about 0.3 is noticeable, and anything above about 0.4 is flagged as too much for UI elements ([wwdcnotes WWDC23 10158](https://wwdcnotes.com/documentation/wwdc23-10158-animate-with-springs/)). Motion's own defaults (tween 0.3 s, spring bounce 0.25) sit inside this envelope, and its `visualDuration` plus `bounce` form maps directly onto Apple's mental model and can be exported as pure CSS ([motion.dev transitions](https://motion.dev/docs/react-transitions)).

The two production curves worth copying verbatim are both Emil's. Vaul's drawer uses `transition: transform 0.5s cubic-bezier(0.32, 0.72, 0, 1)`, a curve taken from Ionic that "closely matches" iOS, with constants of a **25% close threshold, 0.4 velocity threshold, 100 ms scroll-lock** after content returns to top, damped upward over-drag, and momentum-based closing ([emilkowal.ski, drawer](https://emilkowal.ski/ui/building-a-drawer-component); [vaul constants](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/emilkowalski/vaul/main/src/constants.ts)). Sonner's top toast enters from `translateY(-100%)` and opacity 0 over **400 ms**, exits by swipe in 200 ms ease-out, and when collapsed behind newer toasts fades its opacity in 200 ms while its transform takes 500 ms, so the element disappears before it finishes moving ([sonner styles.css](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/emilkowalski/sonner/main/src/styles.css)). That enter-slow, exit-fast asymmetry is corroborated by Material's token structure and by Jakub Krehel's rule that exits should be subtler than enters and every animation must be interruptible ([longlist.design](https://longlist.design/designer/jakub)).

What makes Family, Arc and Flighty feel better than the average app is choreography discipline rather than more motion. Family's stated values are simplicity, fluidity and selective delight; tab switches move in the direction of the tapped tab because "we fly instead of teleport," every tray has a different height so transitions read clearly, each tray holds exactly one action and has a title plus a dismiss icon, and delight is spent on rare moments because its impact drops with repetition ([benji.org](https://benji.org/family-values)). Arc's mobile designer argues that "physicality is like a shortcut to joy" and that a button should have "a sharp press" ([Inverse](https://www.inverse.com/gear/arc-web-browser-mobile-app-skeuomorphism-design)). Flighty's founder wants the app to feel "almost boringly obvious" and says "we really have to shine when things go awry" ([Apple Behind the Design](https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=970ncww4)); a third-party teardown reconstructs its status model as green/amber/red capsule badges with a spring of response 0.3 and warning banners at a tighter 8 pt radius than the 12 pt cards, values that are a reconstruction, not Flighty's spec ([blakecrosley.com](https://blakecrosley.com/guides/design/flighty)). Linear, notably, published nothing about motion at all; its redesign post covers LCH color, Inter Display and density ([linear.app](https://linear.app/blog/how-we-redesigned-the-linear-ui)), and interviews attribute its "feels fast" reputation to craft and optimistic local-first behavior rather than animation ([Sequoia](https://sequoiacap.com/article/linear-spotlight/)). On the web that translates to Vercel's rule to update the UI immediately when success is likely and reconcile later ([Vercel guidelines](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/vercel-labs/web-interface-guidelines/main/README.md)).

The press recipe for a single prominent button is unusually consistent: `scale(0.97)` on `:active` (range 0.95–0.98) with about a 160 ms ease-out, never on disabled elements, and in Motion the `transform: 'scale(0.97)'` form rather than the `scale` shorthand, which is reported not to be hardware-accelerated under load ([emilkowalski/skills summary](https://instagit.com/emilkowalski/skills/button-press-feedback-with-scale-transform/)). Vercel adds that active states must have more contrast than rest, that loading must keep the original label with a spinner that has a **150–300 ms show delay and a 300–500 ms minimum visible time**, that mutations should complete under 500 ms, and that `touch-action: manipulation` should be set to kill the double-tap zoom delay ([Vercel guidelines](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/vercel-labs/web-interface-guidelines/main/README.md)). Haptics do not transfer to the web (iOS Safari has never supported `navigator.vibrate`), so physicality has to come from press scale, contrast change and a low-bounce release. The anti-patterns are equally consistent: `transition: all`, animating width/height/padding, ease-in or linear for UI, animations that block input, animations that do not originate from their trigger, gratuitous bounce, spinners that flash, and animating actions that repeat many times a day ([emilkowal.ski](https://emilkowal.ski/ui/great-animations); [rauno.me](https://rauno.me/craft/interaction-design); [Apple HIG Motion](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/motion)).

## Dark basemaps carry hierarchy in gray steps, with one blue route

Every inspectable premium dark style shares a structure. CARTO Dark Matter uses a `#0e0e0e` background, slate-blue `#2C353C` water, blue-gray primary roads at `rgba(83,86,102,1)`, and parks that are the same color as land, effectively invisible ([CARTO dark-matter.json](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/CartoDB/basemap-styles/master/mapboxgl/dark-matter.json)). Protomaps' `dark` flavor uses `#1f1f1f` earth, `#31353f` water, `#111111` buildings (darker than land), and a road ramp of **`#333333` → `#3d3d3d` → `#474747`** with all casings at the earth color, so roads read as light strokes separated by dark gaps ([protomaps flavors.ts](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/protomaps/basemaps/main/styles/src/flavors.ts)). OpenFreeMap `dark`, the only fully keyless option, is the most austere: `rgb(12,12,12)` land, `rgb(27,27,29)` water, buildings at `rgb(10,10,10)` with a `rgb(27,27,29)` outline for an etched look, and an inverted road treatment where motorway interiors are `#000` on a `rgba(60,60,60,0.8)` casing, with Noto Sans uppercase labels at `rgb(101,101,101)` ([OpenFreeMap dark style](https://tiles.openfreemap.org/styles/dark)). That label gray computes to only about **3.4:1** against its own background, which is why the note's instruction to lift labels is not optional for an outdoor app. Google's canonical night palette (base `#242f3e`, roads `#38414e`, water `#17263c`) is bluer and lighter, and its 2023 shift to grays drew criticism from a former Google Maps designer as "computer generated," with a specific complaint that active and alternate routes "look too similar in night mode" ([Google style array](https://developers.google.com/maps/documentation/javascript/examples/style-array); [Map Room Blog](https://www.maproomblog.com/2023/11/thoughts-on-google-maps-new-design-from-a-former-google-maps-designer/); [9to5Google](https://9to5google.com/2023/11/25/android-auto-google-maps-new-colors/)). Uber's and Apple's actual map palettes are closed; Uber's dark-mode post describes theme tokens and a hex linter but never mentions the map ([Uber blog](https://www.uber.com/blog/from-light-to-dark-the-story-behind-dark-mode/)), so any "Uber at night" comparison is by visual analogy, and the closest open analogues are Protomaps `dark` and CARTO Dark Matter.

The only vendor-published route recipe is Mapbox Navigation's: main route **`#56A8FB` with casing `#2F7AC6`**, alternates `#999999` over `#666666`, round caps and joins, casing width 1.2 times the route width, widths on a linear zoom interpolate, and optional `line-blur` glow layers underneath ([Mapbox iOS route styling](https://docs.mapbox.com/ios/navigation/v2/examples/route-lines-styling/); [Mapbox Android RouteLineViewDynamicOptions](https://docs.mapbox.com/android/navigation/api/coreframework/3.21.13/ui-maps/com.mapbox.navigation.ui.maps.route.line.model/-mapbox-route-line-view-dynamic-options-builder/index.html)). The MapLibre pattern is the same three stacked layers, casing under fill, with `line-blur` and round caps available on the line layer ([MapLibre Flutter line layer](https://maplibre.org/flutter-maplibre-gl/layers/line-layer/)). That blue is the industry default because it sits far from red/amber alert hues and from any green park tint, and the contrast check confirms `#56A8FB` reads at roughly **7.8:1** on `#0c0c0c`, comfortably above the 3:1 non-text floor.

Color for urgency has real research behind it, and it argues for restraint. Red is consistently the strongest urgency signal (highest-rated at fast blink; ranked above orange on a danger scale), orange is associated with impulsivity, and yellow and orange are hard to tell apart, so they should never be adjacent tiers ([Springer](https://www.springerprofessional.de/the-color-and-blink-frequency-of-led-notification-lights-and-sma/2171326); [White Rose eprints](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/123161/); [Regensburg](https://epub.uni-regensburg.de/33832/)). Material's dark-theme guidance is that saturated accents fail 4.5:1 on dark surfaces and should be lifted, turning error `#B00020` into `#CF6679` via a 40% white overlay, with surfaces starting at `#121212` ([Material dark theme](https://m2.material.io/design/color/dark-theme)). USWDS tells government sites to "avoid heavy use of bright red or orange, since these colors can provoke fear or panic," to use a full-width alert near the top, and never to stack alerts ([USWDS site alert](https://designsystem.digital.gov/components/site-alert/)). Citizen's own designers test colors "in all situations, at all times of day" and code severity green/yellow/red ([AIGA](https://eyeondesign.aiga.org/do-crime-tracking-apps-have-a-design-problem/)). Protomaps' dark-flavor accent set (`#F2567A` red, `#F19B6E` tangerine, `#30C573` green, `#4299BB` blue) is already tuned against `#1f1f1f` land ([protomaps flavors.ts](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/protomaps/basemaps/main/styles/src/flavors.ts)), and the contrast check in this report finds those reds and ambers land between 5:1 and 9:1 as text on near-black, while **white text on any of those fills scores only 2.2–3.8:1 and fails AA**. The design consequence is firm: severity is expressed as colored text, icon and edge on a dark surface, never as white-on-color floods.

Glass overlays are feasible but expensive over a moving WebGL map. Josh Comeau's recipe uses `backdrop-filter: blur(16px)` (12 px for a calmer look), a 0.5–0.6 alpha tint, `saturate(120–140%)` to counter blur's desaturation, and an `@supports` fallback ([Josh Comeau](https://www.joshwcomeau.com/css/backdrop-filter/)); practitioner guidance caps glass at **2–3 surfaces per viewport and 8–15 px blur**, never animates a blurred element, and reduces or disables the effect on touch devices and under `prefers-reduced-transparency` ([OpenReplay glass](https://blog.openreplay.com/create-glassmorphic-ui-css/); [OpenReplay backdrop](https://blog.openreplay.com/creating-blurred-backgrounds-css-backdrop-filter/)). No controlled benchmark exists for backdrop-filter over a map canvas on mobile, and Apple's own Liquid Glass had to be toned down after first-beta complaints that screens felt overcrowded ([MacRumors](https://macrumors.com/guide/ios-26-liquid-glass)), so Haven should treat glass as a single-surface accent with a solid fallback during map motion.

Typography follows the same restraint. Shipped dark basemaps use Montserrat (CARTO), Noto Sans (OpenFreeMap), and historically DIN at Mapbox ([Mapbox blog](https://blog.mapbox.com/mapbox-streets-for-gl-4710a6e1a14f)); Uber Move is proprietary and "inspired by transportation fonts" ([Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2018-09-12-uber-new-typeface.html)); Linear's redesign standardised on Inter Display ([linear.app](https://linear.app/blog/how-we-redesigned-the-linear-ui)). Cartography guidance favors sans-serifs for small-size screen legibility and stresses that hierarchy from size, weight and color matters as much as the face ([atlas.co](https://atlas.co/courses/gis-basics/typography-in-maps/)), and across the dark styles minor labels sit at 9–10 px uppercase with 1 px land-colored halos and text in the `#7a7a7a`–`#d4d4dc` band, never pure white.

## MapLibre reproduces the whole catalogue except native surfaces

Everything in the previous sections has a documented web implementation; what does not transfer is the OS layer. MapLibre GL JS 6.x (ESM-only, WebGL2) ships globe, sky, terrain, `line-gradient`, feature-state at "up to 3.4x faster," a `reduceMotion` map option, and a `flyTo` fix that honors `prefers-reduced-motion` ([MapLibre CHANGELOG](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/maplibre/maplibre-gl-js/main/CHANGELOG.md)). Mapbox GL JS v3 keeps the Standard style's light presets, 3D lighting, and the `line-trim-offset`/`line-trim-color` route-vanish properties that MapLibre lacks ([Mapbox style spec](https://docs.mapbox.com/style-spec/reference/layers/); [Mapbox migrate to v3](https://docs.mapbox.com/mapbox-gl-js/guides/migrate-to-v3/)), but a flat dark city map does not need any of them, and Mapbox charges after **50,000 monthly loads** ([Mapbox pricing](https://www.mapbox.com/pricing)). OpenFreeMap has "no limits on the number of map views or requests," no keys, MIT licensing and commercial use allowed, at the cost of no SLA ([openfreemap.org](https://openfreemap.org/)), which makes it the right default for a free public-safety tool while the lack of SLA argues for the offline fallbacks below.

The route reveal has a canonical GPU-cheap form. Mapbox's own cinematic-routes post drives the reveal with a `step` on `line-progress` inside `line-gradient`, updating one paint property per frame rather than re-tessellating geometry: `["step", ["line-progress"], color, phase, "rgba(0,0,0,0)"]` ([Mapbox blog](https://www.mapbox.com/blog/building-cinematic-route-animations-with-mapboxgl)). This requires a GeoJSON source with `lineMetrics: true` ([MapLibre gradient example](https://maplibre.org/maplibre-gl-js/docs/examples/create-a-gradient-line-using-an-expression/)), works identically in MapLibre, and can be softened from a hard edge into a short ramp with `interpolate`. The camera has sourced defaults even though no shipping app's durations are known: `flyTo` uses `curve` 1.42 from a van Wijk user study and `speed` 1.2 screenfuls per second, `fitBounds` accepts `padding` and `maxZoom`, `AnimationOptions` take `duration`, an `easing` function and an `essential` flag that bypasses reduced motion, and padding persists globally unless `retainPadding: false` ([MapLibre FlyToOptions](https://maplibre.org/maplibre-gl-js/docs/API/type-aliases/FlyToOptions/); [MapLibre AnimationOptions](https://maplibre.org/maplibre-gl-js/docs/API/type-aliases/AnimationOptions/); [Mapbox GL JS API](https://docs.mapbox.com/mapbox-gl-js/api/properties)). The pulsing dot is an official example in both libraries: a 200×200 canvas implementing `StyleImageInterface`, a **1 s cycle** in which the outer ring grows from 30 px to 100 px radius while fading and the inner dot stays solid, registered via `addImage(..., {pixelRatio: 2})` and rendered by a symbol layer that calls `triggerRepaint()` ([MapLibre animated icon](https://maplibre.org/maplibre-gl-js/docs/examples/add-image-animated/); [Mapbox example](https://docs.mapbox.com/mapbox-gl-js/example/add-image-animated)). Google's blue dot adds two semantics worth copying: a light-blue accuracy circle that appears only when location is uncertain and shrinks as confidence rises, and a grey dot that means "no fix, showing last known location" ([Google Maps Help](https://support.google.com/maps/answer/2839911?hl=en)).

Bottom sheets over a map are the one place web implementations diverge. A developer building a map-centric train app evaluated react-spring-bottom-sheet, react-modal-sheet, Vaul and Plain sheet and rejected all of them for "long scrollable content overlaid on a map, multiple snap points, and steady drag on touch devices," finding JS-driven drag prone to jank, and instead used CSS `scroll-snap` with a `pointer-events: none` host so touches on the transparent area reach the map ([dev.to, Ketonen](https://dev.to/viliket/native-like-bottom-sheets-on-the-web-the-power-of-modern-css-ld6)). Vaul remains the standard for a shorter sheet, with its drag applied as a direct `translateY` because per-frame CSS-variable updates caused style-recalc lag ([emilkowal.ski, drawer](https://emilkowal.ski/ui/building-a-drawer-component)). Haven's sheet carries a few rows, not a long list, so Vaul's constants are the right fit, with `touch-action: pan-y`, `overscroll-behavior: contain` and `env(safe-area-inset-bottom)` padding per PWA guidance ([techinterview bottom sheet](https://www.techinterview.org/post/3233475371/build-bottom-sheet-component-mobile-web/)). For React wiring, react-map-gl v8's `react-map-gl/maplibre` entry in uncontrolled `initialViewState` mode avoids a React render on every camera event during a flyTo, and all per-frame work belongs in a `useRef` rAF loop, never in `useState` ([react-map-gl state management](https://visgl.github.io/react-map-gl/docs/get-started/state-management)). Routing has free options with explicit terms: OSRM's demo server returns `geometries=geojson` directly at **no more than one request per second** and non-commercial use only ([OSRM demo wiki](https://github.com/Project-OSRM/osrm-backend/wiki/Demo-server)), while OpenRouteService's free plan gives **2,000 directions per day at 40 per minute** and a 10,000/day Collaborative plan for humanitarian, academic, governmental and non-profit use ([openrouteservice plans](https://openrouteservice.org/plans/)), the tier a Wisconsin shelter tool should apply for.

Native-only features that must be substituted rather than imitated: Live Activities and Dynamic Island trip tracking ([RideGuru](https://ride.guru/content/newsroom/ubers-new-redesign-explained)), Liquid Glass refraction and iOS detent dimming ([Expo blog](https://expo.dev/blog/how-to-create-apple-maps-style-liquid-glass-sheets)), haptic ticks, Critical Alerts that break through silent mode ([NERV App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/nerv-disaster-prevention/id1472338480)), and OS permission dialogs. The web substitutes are `backdrop-filter`, press scale, `theme-color` meta interpolation so Safari's chrome matches a banner (Vaul's technique), and voice readout via the Web Speech API, which has a direct precedent in Yurekuru Call adding voice warnings at the request of visually impaired users ([Kyodo PR](https://kyodonewsprwire.jp/release/201803091757)).

## Emergency UX wins by tiers, geofences, and honesty about GPS

The reputational risk in this category is a wrong or missing alert, not an ugly screen. Watch Duty is praised for reporting fires "before news stations" and for "only notifies me of real fires," while its complaints are markers "miles off," alerts for other states, evacuation-zone fills that make streets "really really hard to see," and alerts "not nearly loud enough" ([JustUseApp](https://justuseapp.com/en/app/1574452924/watch-duty-wildfire/reviews)); it gained roughly **2.5 million users in seven days** during the Palisades and Eaton fires ([Fox LA](https://www.foxla.com/news/watch-duty-la-fires-california)). FEMA app users report the same alert arriving roughly hourly; Red Cross users report duplicate notifications and lost radius settings ([FEMA App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/fema/id474807486); [chrome-stats](https://chrome-stats.com/d/id954783878)). The pattern that avoids this is tiered and geofenced: Android earthquake alerts use two tiers, "Be Aware" and a full-screen "Take Action" that fires only for MMI 5+ and breaks through Do Not Disturb ([9to5Google](https://9to5google.com/?p=733492)); NERV sends Critical Alerts only for the top warning levels and only to users inside the target area ([NERV App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/nerv-disaster-prevention/id1472338480)); Google gates SOS Alerts through a dedicated team and shows a single crisis card on the map with a structured sheet beneath it ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/?p=1518291); [Google Maps Help](https://support.google.com/maps/answer/9985621)). NN/g's finding that alerts which all look the same get treated as equally urgent ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/smart-home-notifications/)) and healthcare alarm-fatigue research on near-identical signals ([Halo Lab](https://www.halo-lab.com/blog/alert-fatigue-in-healthcare)) both say three visibly different tiers are the maximum. FEMA's WEA template (source, event, location, action, URL/phone, in 90 or 360 characters, no local acronyms, Spanish version) is a ready-made content constraint for the banner ([FEMA IPAWS guide](https://www.fema.gov/sites/default/files/documents/fema_ipaws-guide-constructing-wea-eas-messages_1.pdf)).

Location failure is handled best by apps that never dead-end. FEMA accepts up to five ZIP codes and Notify NYC up to five saved addresses as substitutes for device location ([FEMA App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/fema/id474807486); [NYC EM](https://www.nyc.gov/site/em/about/press-releases/20170922_pr_nycem-doitt-release-notify-nyc-app.page)); guidance for location-dependent apps is that a denial "shouldn't just break or present an error" but offer manual entry with a reason ([Arkansas DEQ guide](https://tap-app-api.adeq.arkansas.gov/post/user-location-data-a-guide)); Apple's review expectation is that any pre-permission screen leads straight into the system prompt ([Apple Developer Forums](https://origin-devforums.apple.com/forums/profile/velkadamban)). Honesty about accuracy is itself a trust mechanism: Google states wildfire outlines "may differ from actual fire extent by several miles" ([Google Maps Help](https://support.google.com/maps/answer/9985621)), and what3words' offline compass mode, which computes bearing and distance on device, is the model for what to show when routing is unreachable ([what3words App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/what3words/id657878530)). None of the named apps is a PWA, and the only documented offline behaviours are narrow (what3words compass, FEMA cached preparedness content, Noonlight explicitly requiring live data), so Haven's offline design is a synthesis rather than a copy: precache the shell and regional shelter list, and degrade to text plus bearing.

The accessibility floors are hard numbers and, for a stressed outdoor user, are floors rather than targets. WCAG 2.2 requires **24×24 CSS px** targets at AA, 4.5:1 text contrast (7:1 at AAA), 3:1 for UI components, text resizable to 200%, and adjustable timing for any countdown ([WCAG 2.2 quick reference](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/quickref/?versions=2.2&levels=aaa); [Understanding 2.5.8](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/target-size-minimum.html)); Apple asks for 44 pt and 11 pt text, Android for 48 dp with 8 dp spacing ([Apple design tips](https://developer.apple.com/design/tips/); [Android accessibility](https://support.google.com/accessibility/android/answer/7101858)). Smashing's stress guidance describes cognitive tunneling, weaker reading comprehension and poorer fine motor control under stress, and prescribes one action per screen, 10–30 second steps, pre-filled defaults, and testing in noisy real environments ([Smashing Magazine](https://smashingmagazine.com/2025/11/designing-for-stress-emergency/)). NERV's adjustable contrast, text size and weight, color-blind-safe palettes and an explicit Dark/Light theme choice (most users picked Dark in a 2023 survey) are the accessibility reference ([NERV App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/nerv-disaster-prevention/id1472338480)). Outdoor readability is a contrast problem, not a brightness problem ([Field Technologies Online](https://www.fieldtechnologiesonline.com/doc/what-really-makes-a-mobile-device-screen-readable-outdoors-0001)); one CityStrides user found dark maps "harder to read" in sun, especially thin purple lines, while another praised the same contrast ([CityStrides community](https://community.citystrides.com/t/dark-mode-for-supporters/26979?page=2)), and a CoMaps discussion separates a low-contrast "night" style from a high-contrast "dark" style usable in daylight ([CoMaps wiki](https://zff.dev/comaps/om-issues-wiki/issues/688)). The honest position is that a dark map is the right default for brand, OLED and parity with every reference product, provided its strokes and labels are lifted well above the stock values, and that a one-tap high-contrast light theme must exist because no mobile-web ambient-light API is reliable.

## Design spec for Haven

Each item below names its source; items marked *derived* are this report's synthesis from the cited constraints, not a value measured from a shipping app.

### Single-screen layout and states

The screen is a full-bleed map with three overlay regions and three states. The top region holds the severity banner (full width, non-blocking, overlaying the map rather than pushing it, per USWDS placement and the Sonner overlay pattern ([USWDS](https://designsystem.digital.gov/components/site-alert/); [sonner](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/emilkowalski/sonner/main/src/styles.css))). The right edge holds exactly one floating round control, locate-me, at 48 px with high-contrast fill, because Citymapper's low-contrast floating control is the documented failure case ([IXD@Pratt](https://ixd.prattsi.org/2026/02/design-critique-citymapper-ios-app/)). The bottom region is the sheet. In the **idle** state the sheet is a peek containing a grabber and the primary button only; the map shows the user dot with accuracy ring, hazard polygons, and the nearest shelter pin, nothing else (Watch Duty clutter complaint; Lyft POI removal ([JustUseApp](https://justuseapp.com/en/app/1574452924/watch-duty-wildfire/reviews); [Lyft blog](https://www.lyft.com/blog/posts/lyfts-secret-plan-to-take-control-of-its-maps-and-its-future))). In the **routing** state (after the tap, while the API responds) the button keeps its label and shows a spinner after 150 ms for at least 300 ms, with width and height locked ([Vercel guidelines](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/vercel-labs/web-interface-guidelines/main/README.md)). In the **guiding** state the sheet rises to the half detent with shelter name, distance, walking time, address, a `tel:` link and a "Change shelter" secondary action, while the camera fits the route with bottom padding equal to the sheet height; chrome follows phase exactly as Apple's compact turn bar and Google's bottom mode row do ([MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/guide/ios-26-maps); [9to5Google](https://9to5google.com/2024/02/07/google-maps-directions-search-redesign/)).

The primary button is full width, **64 px tall** (derived from Smashing's motor-control findings, above the 44/48 floors), labelled "Take me to the nearest shelter," filled `#f5f5f5` with `#0c0c0c` text (**17.9:1**, computed) so it is the brightest object on screen and readable in sun, with `touch-action: manipulation`, `-webkit-tap-highlight-color: transparent`, and `:active` darkening to `#e6e6e6`. It fires on a single tap with no hold and no countdown, because routing is non-destructive; any future "Call 911" or "Alert contacts" action gets press-and-hold with a cancelable countdown and lives visually apart from it ([Apple support HT209053](https://support.apple.com/HT209053); [NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/error-prevention/)). The button is deliberately not red: USWDS warns heavy red provokes panic, and the contrast check shows white text on red fails AA.

Sheet detents are **0.12 / 0.5 / 0.92** of viewport height (derived from Apple Maps' 0.1 / 0.5 / 1.0 with the top capped so a map sliver remains, per Google's status-bar strip ([Expo blog](https://expo.dev/blog/how-to-create-apple-maps-style-liquid-glass-sheets); [9to5Google](https://9to5google.com/2025/03/07/google-maps-iphone-sheet-redesign/))). The grabber is always visible, drag-to-dismiss is disabled, there is no scrim below the half detent, and the sheet body uses `touch-action: pan-y`, `overscroll-behavior: contain`, and `padding-bottom: env(safe-area-inset-bottom)` ([Sarunw](https://sarunw.com/posts/bottom-sheet-in-ios-15-with-uisheetpresentationcontroller/); [techinterview](https://www.techinterview.org/post/3233475371/build-bottom-sheet-component-mobile-web/)). Each state's sheet has a visibly different height so transitions read (Family's tray rule ([benji.org](https://benji.org/family-values))).

### Dark map style

Start from OpenFreeMap `dark` (`https://tiles.openfreemap.org/styles/dark`) and apply `setPaintProperty` overrides after `load`, or fork the JSON in Maputnik ([OpenFreeMap style](https://tiles.openfreemap.org/styles/dark); [openfreemap-styles](https://github.com/niyamaka/openfreemap-styles)). Keep land at `rgb(12,12,12)` and buildings darker than land with the `rgb(27,27,29)` outline (the etched look OpenFreeMap and Protomaps share). Replace the inverted road treatment with a Protomaps-style lifted ramp, *derived* from the `#333`→`#3d3d3d`→`#474747` structure: minor `#3a3a3a`, major `#444444`, highway `#505050`, casings at land color ([protomaps flavors.ts](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/protomaps/basemaps/main/styles/src/flavors.ts)). Shift water to a desaturated blue-gray `#2a3038` (between CARTO's `#2C353C` and Protomaps' `#31353f`). Lift place and road labels from `rgb(101,101,101)` to **`#bdbdbd`** (10.4:1 computed) with the existing 1 px `rgba(0,0,0,0.7)` halo, keep the uppercase Noto Sans treatment, and dim rather than hide labels as zoom increases, as CARTO does with country labels ([CARTO](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/CartoDB/basemap-styles/master/mapboxgl/dark-matter.json)). Set `fadeDuration: 0` on the map so labels do not pop during the camera fit ([MapLibre MapOptions](https://maplibre.org/maplibre-gl-js/docs/API/type-aliases/MapOptions)). Hazard polygons follow Google's convention: red fill at low opacity (`#F2567A` at 0.18, *derived* opacity) for confirmed areas and yellow (`#F19B6E` at 0.14) for uncertain ones, with no outline heavier than 1.5 px so streets stay legible under them ([Google Maps Help](https://support.google.com/maps/answer/9985621); [JustUseApp Watch Duty](https://justuseapp.com/en/app/1574452924/watch-duty-wildfire/reviews)). Keep the layer budget to the basemap plus three route layers, one symbol layer for the dot and pin, one fill layer for hazards, and no 3D buildings ([MapLibre 3D example](https://maplibre.org/maplibre-gl-js/docs/examples/display-buildings-in-3d/) exists but adds nothing for this task).

### Route-reveal recipe

Add the route as a GeoJSON source with `lineMetrics: true`, then three line layers with round caps and joins in this order: `route-glow` (`line-color #56A8FB`, `line-opacity 0.3`, `line-blur 10`, width 2.5× fill), `route-casing` (`#2F7AC6`, width 1.2× fill), and `route-fill` (`#56A8FB`, width `["interpolate", ["linear"], ["zoom"], 10, 3, 13, 4, 16, 6, 19, 10]`) ([Mapbox route styling](https://docs.mapbox.com/ios/navigation/v2/examples/route-lines-styling/); [MapLibre gradient](https://maplibre.org/maplibre-gl-js/docs/examples/create-a-gradient-line-using-an-expression/)); the glow opacity, blur and width multipliers are *derived* from Mapbox's blur options, not vendor defaults. On data arrival, run `fitBounds` as specified, then at 200 ms into the camera move start a rAF loop of **900 ms** (matching the camera, *derived*) that sets `line-gradient` on `route-fill` to `["interpolate", ["linear"], ["line-progress"], max(0, phase - 0.03), "#56A8FB", phase, "rgba(86,168,251,0)"]`, the Mapbox Impact Tools step technique softened with a 3% ramp ([Mapbox blog](https://www.mapbox.com/blog/building-cinematic-route-animations-with-mapboxgl)). Casing and glow are drawn at full length immediately so the user sees the whole path's silhouette before the bright fill arrives; this is a *derived* choice that keeps the information visible even if the animation is interrupted. When the fill reaches the destination, run the shelter pin's pulse for three 1 s cycles, then hold it static. Under reduced motion, skip the loop and set the gradient to its final state. The minimum fill width of 4 px at z15+ with a dark casing is the sunlight floor the dark-map note calls for.

### User-dot recipe

Implement the dot as a `StyleImageInterface` canvas image on a symbol layer, 200×200 at `pixelRatio: 2`, with a solid inner dot of radius 30 px (at ratio 2, so 15 CSS px) in `#56A8FB` with a 2 px white stroke, and an outer ring that grows from 30 px to 100 px over a **1 s cycle** at `rgba(86,168,251, 1 - t)` ([MapLibre animated icon](https://maplibre.org/maplibre-gl-js/docs/examples/add-image-animated/)). The canonical example uses coral; blue is substituted here so the dot shares the route hue and stays far from alert reds. Add a separate `circle` layer for the accuracy ring, visible only when `coords.accuracy > 25 m` (*derived* threshold), radius driven by accuracy in meters via a zoom-aware expression, fill `rgba(86,168,251,0.12)` with a 1 px `rgba(86,168,251,0.4)` stroke, so it shrinks as confidence rises exactly as Google's does ([Google Maps Help](https://support.google.com/maps/answer/2839911?hl=en)). A heading cone appears only when `DeviceOrientation` data is present, as a 60-degree sector at 0.25 opacity (*derived*; Google's beam-width-equals-compass-accuracy behavior is reported only by Geoawesome ([Geoawesome](https://geoawesome.com/google-maps-get-redesign-of-the-blue-dot-showing-your-position))). When the fix is older than 60 s or lost, swap the image to a grey `#9a9a9a` static dot and show "Last known, 2 min ago" in the sheet. Under reduced motion the ring is static at 50 px. Position updates between GPS samples are lerped in the rAF loop over the sample interval, the technique every ride-hail reproduction uses ([UberCarAnimation](https://github.com/pranaypatel512/UberCarAnimation)).

### Severity banner rules

There are exactly three tiers mapped to NWS wording so the app matches TV and WEA language: **Advisory** (Watch), **Warning**, and **Take Action** (Emergency). Only one banner is ever on screen; a new higher tier replaces a lower one, and nothing stacks ([USWDS](https://designsystem.digital.gov/components/site-alert/)). The banner is a dark surface (`rgba(18,18,20,0.92)`) with a 4 px left edge, a 20 px icon, a capsule label and text, all in the tier color, never a solid colored flood, because white-on-tier-color fails AA and USWDS warns against heavy red ([Material dark theme](https://m2.material.io/design/color/dark-theme)). Advisory uses `#9a9a9a`, `role="status"`, no sound, and collapses to a single line. Warning uses `#F19B6E`, `role="status"`, and a 400 ms slide from `translateY(-100%)`. Take Action uses `#F2567A`, `role="alert"` with `aria-live="assertive"`, the same 400 ms entrance, a `theme-color` tint, a single Web Speech readout of the headline, and a 48 px tall tappable area that expands into a Google-style crisis sheet with About, What to do, and Share ([Google Maps Help](https://support.google.com/maps/answer/9985621)). Content follows the WEA template, source plus hazard plus place plus action plus time, capped at 360 characters with no local acronyms and a Spanish toggle ([FEMA IPAWS guide](https://www.fema.gov/sites/default/files/documents/fema_ipaws-guide-constructing-wea-eas-messages_1.pdf)). Every tier carries an icon and a text label so color is never the only channel (WCAG 1.4.1), and banners are removed when resolved, never visually hidden and revealed later. Push or sound belongs only to Take Action and only when the user's position or saved address is inside the warned polygon ([NERV App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/nerv-disaster-prevention/id1472338480); [9to5Google](https://9to5google.com/?p=733492)).

### Location-denied and offline fallbacks

Request location only at the first tap, with one sentence ("We use your location once to find the closest shelter") that leads directly into the browser prompt ([Apple Developer Forums](https://origin-devforums.apple.com/forums/profile/velkadamban)). The ladder, in order: precise GPS; coarse position shown with the accuracy ring and a "Approximate location" label; a saved address (up to five, mirroring FEMA and Notify NYC) ([FEMA App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/fema/id474807486); [NYC EM](https://www.nyc.gov/site/em/about/press-releases/20170922_pr_nycem-doitt-release-notify-nyc-app.page)); a drop-a-pin mode with a 64 px "Use this spot" button; and finally a county or city picker that lists the nearest shelters for that region with the sheet title "Location off, showing shelters near Madison." The primary button is never disabled by a denial; its label changes to "Choose where you are" and the sheet explains why in one line ([Arkansas DEQ guide](https://tap-app-api.adeq.arkansas.gov/post/user-location-data-a-guide)). The grey "last known" dot and a "last updated" timestamp are always shown when the fix is stale ([Google Maps Help](https://support.google.com/maps/answer/2839911?hl=en)).

Offline and low-signal are the OpenFreeMap no-SLA and OSRM one-request-per-second risks made concrete, so the service worker precaches the app shell, the Wisconsin shelter list, and low-zoom tiles around saved addresses (within OpenFreeMap's 3,000-tile / 50 MB download limit ([openfreemap.org](https://openfreemap.org/))). If the routing request fails or takes longer than 4 s (*derived* timeout), the app computes straight-line distance and bearing on device and shows a compass card, what3words style, with the shelter name, address, distance, bearing arrow and `tel:` link, labelled "Offline, showing direction only" ([what3words App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/what3words/id657878530)). This text card survives at 2G and satellite bandwidth, the one mode Apple's SOS confirms works when nothing else does ([Apple support](https://support.apple.com/en-us/101573)). Never show a bare spinner for a failed route; skeletons are used only when load exceeds 1 s and must mirror the final sheet layout ([SAP Fiori](https://experience.sap.com/fiori-design-ios/?p=25849)).

### Accessibility minimums

Targets: 48×48 px with 8 px spacing on every control, 64 px for the primary button, exceeding WCAG 2.2's 24 px, Apple's 44 pt and Android's 48 dp ([Understanding 2.5.8](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/target-size-minimum.html); [Apple design tips](https://developer.apple.com/design/tips/); [Android accessibility](https://support.google.com/accessibility/android/answer/7101858)). Contrast: **7:1** on all banner and sheet text (AAA, because sunlight cuts effective contrast), 4.5:1 everywhere else, 3:1 on the route, dot and hazard edges against the basemap ([WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/quickref/?versions=2.2&levels=aaa)); every value in the color table above meets these. Text resizes to 200% without clipping, browser zoom is never disabled, inputs are at least 16 px ([Vercel guidelines](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/vercel-labs/web-interface-guidelines/main/README.md)). `prefers-reduced-motion` and `prefers-reduced-transparency` are both honored, with the fallbacks in the motion table and a solid sheet. Every map control has an accessible name; the shelter result is announced via a polite live region; the Take Action banner via an assertive one. An explicit Dark/Light toggle lives in the sheet's overflow, with the light theme being a high-contrast variant rather than an inversion, following NERV ([NERV App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/nerv-disaster-prevention/id1472338480)). A voice readout of the shelter name, distance and first instruction is available from the guiding sheet (Yurekuru precedent ([Kyodo PR](https://kyodonewsprwire.jp/release/201803091757))). English and Spanish are both shipped, per FEMA WEA guidance. No countdown exists anywhere in the routing flow, which satisfies WCAG 2.2.1 by construction.

## Conclusion

The research reframes "premium" for this category. The products that feel best are not the ones with the most motion; they are the ones whose information architecture is so reduced that the small amount of motion left has a job to do. That is why the most useful numbers in this report come from component authors and platform token files rather than from the flagship apps themselves: Uber, Apple and Google never publish their camera durations because the durations are not what users notice, while Emil Kowalski, Material and Apple's spring session publish theirs because they are teaching the discipline the big apps practise silently. Haven should therefore treat its motion budget as a constraint to protect, not a feature to showcase, and spend its one moment of visible choreography on the route reveal, where the animation carries real information about where to go.

Two findings should change how the team thinks going forward. First, the contrast arithmetic rules out the obvious "big red emergency button" and "red banner" idioms: white text on every plausible red or amber fails AA, so severity has to live in colored text and edges on dark surfaces, which is also what USWDS and the urgency research recommend on fear-avoidance grounds. Second, the dark map that the brand wants is defensible in sunlight only if its strokes and labels are lifted well above the stock OpenFreeMap values and a high-contrast light theme is one tap away; the anecdotal evidence cuts both ways, no ambient-light API exists on the mobile web, and the cost of being wrong is a stressed person who cannot read the shelter name. The right next step is a field test of the lifted dark style and the light fallback on a mid-range Android phone in direct Wisconsin sun, because that single test will settle more than any further desk research can.
